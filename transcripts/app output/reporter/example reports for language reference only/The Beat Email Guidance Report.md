# The Beat Emails: Behavioral Design Review and Recommendations 
**Date:** January 28, 2026 

## Overall Guidance 
Format and define the main to-do’s of the email so you can’t easily scroll past them. 
Add a visually distinct CTA/key takeaways summary to each section. 
Change the titles of each section to be clearer. 
Transform longer blocks of text into more bulleted lists that are easy to skim. 
Create an easier to parse visual hierarchy. 
The purpose of the yellow highlights may not be obvious to all readers. 
Switch from red titles to black. 
To reduce overwhelm and increase clarity, use visually harmonious methods to draw attention to priority elements. 
Be deliberate and focused in choosing what is a very high priority. 
If too much is flagged as a priority, then it’s hard to know where to start. 

## Chart Design Considerations 
Ideally the charts quickly provide their key message. 
Charts should trigger the behavioral drives to improve performance, while leaving visual breathing room for the other critical action items in the email to stand out. 
However, the current chart style draws a great deal of visual attention, but may not convey their key messages, and discourage action. 
This can easily be improved with a more minimalist design, emphasizing the key insights, and using a few gamification/psychology tricks to drive behavior. 
Go minimalist with chart design. 
Trigger the drive to finish filling bars by making “Needed” sections look “empty” to trigger the Goal-Gradient Effect and Endowed Progress Effect. 
Normalize all bars to the same length to represent “100% of goal met,” leveling the playing field to foster social proof and competition. 
In some cases, consider adding an “All” bar to charts to boost another type of social encouragement - not let the group down by falling short. 
This also helps in encouraging a collaborative win. 
If you want to increase competition further, you can put special decorations on the region that is currently in the lead (like fire, or just a highlight color). 
You can also sort charts from lowest performer to highest performer. 
In cases where someone is really leading in a particular chart, use that as a hook into content about new strategies. 
For example: “How did they DO that? Find out here…” 

### Minimalist and Alternate Chart Design Examples 
“Needed” made more empty feeling. 
Normalizing charts to 100% to feel fair and stir competition and social proof. 
“All” bar added at the bottom to foster feelings of “doing your part” and not letting people down in reaching to national goal.

## Before and After Guidance 
*(This is not an exhaustive redesign, it’s to give some targeted suggestions.)* 

### Main To-Do’s of Whole Email 
**Before:** Features a colored background, to-do checklist, due date + teaser. 
**Critique:** Yellow, red and bold is hard to parse. 
**After:** Make this a bigger/bolder font so you don’t scroll past it.  For normal emphasis, use traditional black bolding.  Leave room for the “must do’s” in the email to really stand out. 

### Section Titles & Task Lists 
**Before:** These to-do/top tips are somewhat hidden.  The graph colors could be adjusted to have more contrast.  It’s a bit unclear as to what is “outstanding” and what is “done”.  Very long block of text, especially if you’re in skim mode. 
**After:** Use a motivating title like, “How to Succeed”.  These to-do/top tips are very clear and easy to parse.  Use a clear title.  Concise, bulleted to-do’s are easier to skim and act on. 

### Chart Gamification 
Charts gamified in two ways: 
    * **1. Drive to finish filling the bar:** “Needed” sections look “empty” to trigger Goal-Gradient Effect and Endowed Progress Effect. 
    * **2. Normalized bars:** All bars normalized to the same length representing “100% of goal met”.  This levels the playing field to foster social proof & competition.
