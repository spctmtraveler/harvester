*More details:*

* **Role and Responsibilities at AJ** Shaunda Reed, a senior development director with AJ for three and a half years, manages two Heartwalk events: one in Indianapolis in the fall and one in Evansville in the spring. This dual timeline creates a collision between March and August, requiring them to manage different goals for different seasons. They manage a team that includes a development coordinator, who handles logistics, office management, and data, and a development director, who focuses on fundraising and fiscal year goals.

* **ELT Recruitment Flow** The first grand step in the Executive Leadership Team (ELT) process is prospecting for leadership, which ideally should happen all year. The process starts with recruiting and confirming the chair, who then assists in recruiting the 15 or more ELT members. Once these components are in place, the campaign officially kicks off with the first leadership team meeting.

* **Chair Recruitment Timing Strategy** While the best practice is to have the event chair for three years, Shaunda Reed has experienced acquisition times ranging from six months to over a year. They highlighted a recent success in recruiting the 2027 chair over a year in advance, which was considered unprecedented and provided the benefit of time for acclimation. The successful strategy for securing busy, powerful leaders involves starting conversations early and pitching future years like 2028 or 2029 if the current year is not feasible, allowing them to work backward and secure leadership for multiple years out.

* **Prospecting for New Leaders** When hunting for potential leaders, Shaunda Reed's team looks at top companies, growing leaders, and encourages diversification outside of healthcare. They also leverage current chairs for valuable input and ask those who previously declined to reconsider.

* **Information Continuity and Salesforce Adoption** Shaunda Reed acknowledged that Salesforce, which was recently introduced, could improve seamlessness in tracking details, but the team is still working out the kinks of documentation and where notes should be stored. The ideal location for documenting conversations about future chair roles and other important notes is within the contact record in Salesforce, as they are trying to move away from spreadsheets for better information continuity, especially because Shaunda Reed is preparing for a maternity leave.

* **Need for a Comprehensive Campaign Report in Salesforce** Shaunda Reed expressed a wish for a report in Salesforce similar to the "First Things First Report" used in Tableau, which provided an at-a-glance view of the complex Heartwalk campaign. This Tableau report synthesized information on multiple components like sponsorship status, at-risk renewals, coach registration, and top walker status. The national team is currently working on developing such a report for Salesforce.

* **SharePoint Organization and Local Customization** The team manages the challenge of finding information in SharePoint by locally structuring their market's SharePoint by campaigns and folders to ensure team members know where to look for documents. They adopt a premise that materials should be easy to locate, even by a "dummy," so they organize resources like flyers under promotional marketing instead of company folders. When national resources are difficult to find or links are broken, they pull those resources down to their local market's SharePoint to ensure team access.

* **Challenges with ELT Activity and External Changes** Shaunda Reed has observed that ELT activity has become somewhat harder due to external changes in how corporations handle sponsorship and funding, such as a shift from discretionary funds to processes requiring portals. They also noted that leaders acting as executive champions in inspiring people to join can sometimes be perceived by companies as persuading or influencing, which has required being creative in their approach.

* **Simplification and Tailoring of National Resources** There is a need to simplify and tailor national resources for ELT members, as they often receive decks full of information but may only need a few slides for a conversation. Shaunda Reed has seen positive steps in this direction, such as a company planner being reduced from eight pages to four. They also shared that they utilize "impact plans" to take notes on behalf of leaders, summarizing meetings, next steps, and brainstorming to assist with follow-up.

* **Need for Diverse Goal-Setting Resources** Shaunda Reed highlighted the need for more diverse goal-setting resources for different industries beyond the current strong tailoring towards healthcare systems. They suggested creating goal-setting examples for banks, small businesses, or manufacturing to reflect the need for company diversification. They also lean on sister markets like Cleveland, Cincinnati, and Columbus for tips and tricks that work with non-healthcare companies.

* **Need for Text Communication System and Day-of Activations** Shaunda Reed mentioned the need for a national text communication system for mass communication, emphasizing that "text is king," especially for event updates like weather delays for the outdoor Heartwalk. For sponsorship, they indicated a need for more diversification in day-of event activations, particularly at the Level Three tier, as they only have about seven options.

* **Analysis of "The Beat" Email** Shaunda Reed acknowledged that "The Beat" email is very good but also very long, which requires intentional reading to avoid missing key information and resources like new templates. They often read it themselves to pull out relevant, smaller chunks for their team. They suggested potentially breaking "The Beat" into spring and fall editions to better consolidate and target information.

* **Local Visualization Tool for Sponsorships** The Indianapolis market created a visualization chart to track and manage signature and platform sponsorships, which helps their campaign team visualize bigger opportunities and track outstanding asks. Robin Krieglstein requested pictures of this board and examples of modified documents.

* **Arming ELT Members with Templates** Shaunda Reed's market arms ELT members for outreach by scripting templates for communications regarding sponsorship, participation, or gifts. They provide these templates via email to the leaders for easy copy-and-paste, with the caveat that they should feel free to customize them. They also create short scripts for calls and utilize text templates for quick action during meetings, viewing this as a one-two punch approach.

* **Desire for More Centralized Templates** Shaunda Reed expressed a need for more readily available templates on the national SharePoint, including welcome messages and re-recruitment messages for executive heart, beyond the current eight-week campaign blitz templates. They believe making templates more accessible and allowing for local customization would help ensure leaders take action. Shaunda Reed suggested that a system where local markets could easily contribute and share templates would be beneficial.

* **Call for Regular National and Regional Gatherings** Shaunda Reed concluded by expressing a wish for more regular national or regional in-person gatherings for Heartwalk staff beyond boot camp. They highlighted that the complexity and high turnover rate in Heartwalk make it important to build face-to-name relationships and maintain inspiration throughout their AHA career.

* **Idea for a Two-Way Sharing Tool** Robin Krieglstein presented the idea of a company-wide tool for associates to share tips and strategies, coupled with a feature to celebrate each other's victories. Shaunda Reed liked the idea, suggesting it could help bond staff and encourage the collection of valuable information.
